# Chen 2025 conventional RC beam: independent-laboratory source, admission held

The conventional cast, tied-cage `CM-C` beam in Chen et al., *A novel in-process rebar integration method for 3D printing reinforced concrete beams and performance evaluation*, is a second-laboratory source candidate for the RC flexure roadmap. Its [versioned Mendeley Data record](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/5y8dzgpg9j/1), DOI `10.17632/5y8dzgpg9j.1`, identifies Qingdao Agricultural University and declares **CC BY 4.0 for the dataset**. The related [publisher article](https://doi.org/10.1080/17452759.2025.2536556) describes `CM-C` as the conventionally cast beam with an ordinary tied reinforcement cage, alongside three printed or assembled-cage variants. Only `CM-C` is considered here. The dataset record and original workbook were checked on 2026-09-29.

This is a source-only intake. **One experimental specimen, 13,480 ordered numeric pairs, zero admitted training rows, zero locked physical-evaluation rows, and zero fixed-parameter solver comparisons** are recorded. The Qingdao/Southeast University study is institutionally and geometrically distinct from the Delft He/4TU reference beams, but independence of laboratories alone does not make a physical comparison valid.

## Physical crosswalk still required

The article describes four-point bending of nominal `1100 × 195 × 210 mm` beams, a `CM-C` ordinary tied cage with 10 mm HRB400 reinforcement, 30 mm protective cover, a 9 kN setup preload, staged displacement loading and five LVDTs at the midpoint, loading points and supports. Its Table 4 reports steel yield/ultimate strengths of 425/601 MPa and 16% elongation. These are published program details, not a verified as-built bar-coordinate schedule, CM-C-specific coupon record or a complete solver material law. The article reports predominantly vertical flexural cracking for `CM-C`, while its other variants introduce printed-layer or assembled-joint behavior. Current axial-curvature fiber-beam physics does not establish the effect of shear deformation, bond slip, local bearing or support motion on this short beam's measured curve.

Before any fixed-parameter comparison, the original setup and data-acquisition definitions must resolve: (1) whether `Load` denotes actuator total or each loading-nose force; (2) which LVDT and support correction produced the workbook plot-x channel, including preloading and offset treatment; (3) support/load station coordinates and restraint details for `CM-C`; (4) bar centroids, stirrup locations and specimen/batch-linked concrete and steel measurements; and (5) the response range in which missing shear, bond and support physics are acceptable. The source's high-frequency plot-x reversals require a **pre-solver** row-selection or interpolation rule, uncertainty model and comparison metric. No factor of two, axis shift, material parameter or smooth curve has been chosen to improve a solver fit.

`CM-C` can supply a separate-study candidate after these gates, but this one conventional specimen cannot be used both to tune material parameters and to claim independent generalization. A freeze of model parameters, eligibility range, source-row transformation, success threshold, and an additional held-out project or specimen group must precede any service-level validation claim. The conventional source remains **HOLD** until then.
