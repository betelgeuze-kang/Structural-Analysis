Its accompanying [final experimental paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.engstruct.2021.113378) ([open university-hosted PDF](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_1392daf2-866f-4db3-b65d-217b901a3295)) and linked [open conference paper](https://pure.tudelft.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/87496941/P0667.pdf) describe four-point bending of conventional reinforced-concrete reference beams alongside SHCC–concrete hybrid beams. This is a candidate for source review, not an admitted training row or physical-validation result.

## Model fit and unresolved inputs

The [final paper, §2.1 and Fig. 2 (printed pp. 2–3)](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_1392daf2-866f-4db3-b65d-217b901a3295) identifies **one** conventional `Reinforced Concrete` reference among six new-study beams. It gives a `1900 × 150 × 200 mm` beam, a `1500 mm` support span with `200 mm` end overhangs, two load positions `500 mm` apart and `500 mm` from their adjacent supports, `3-Φ8` ribbed bottom bars, `2-Φ8` ribbed top bars, and `Φ8@150` stirrups in the shear spans. The paper says those stirrups were provided to ensure flexural failure; the [linked conference paper](https://pure.tudelft.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/87496941/P0667.pdf) reports approximately `0.54%` longitudinal reinforcement. The new reference was cast with the concrete phase of the hybrid beams (§2.2, printed p. 3). By the paper/workbook labels, this control is the workbook's `Reference Beam - New Study`; the workbook gives no independent specimen ID. Fig. 6a also shows a previous-study reference (§3.2.1, printed p. 5). The earlier reference's shorter plotted deflection range reflects LVDT capacity, not reported premature beam failure.

That stated flexural intent makes the **conventional reference**, rather than the SHCC/interface variants, the closest mechanism screen for the current planar axial–curvature fiber beam. The [corotational element](../../src/structural_analysis/elements/stateful_corotational_fiber_beam2d.py) reuses the axial–curvature section integrator; it does not establish a shear, steel–concrete slip, or SHCC–concrete interface response. The hybrid/debonded specimens therefore need different physics, and even the control's whole curve cannot be claimed as a validated model match from this intake.

The [paper's §2.3 and Fig. 4b (printed p. 4)](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_1392daf2-866f-4db3-b65d-217b901a3295) specify displacement-controlled four-point loading at `0.01 mm/s`, with beam deformation measured in the central constant-moment region by LVDTs on one side and DIC on the other. They do **not** identify which instrument and exact location/datum supplied the workbook's `Deflection`, or whether plotted `Load` is total actuator force or force at each loading nose. The separate `deformation` and crack-width channels must not be substituted for deflection. Row order is available, but a time scale for those rows and machine-compliance correction are not established here. The paper's `49.4 ± 2.2 MPa` concrete cube strength is a test-day program average (§3.1, printed p. 4), not a measured strength assigned to this individual beam. Its `46 MPa` concrete strength and `500/550 MPa` B500-steel strengths are **lattice simulation inputs** (§4.2 and Table 3, printed p. 10), not specimen-specific test results. A specimen-specific cube/batch and measured-steel-property link remains unverified. The four-point setup does not document an axial preload in the inspected source; no axial-load value is inferred.

The next safe step is to seek the original setup/measurement documentation for the total-versus-per-nose force convention, the `Deflection` gauge and datum, and the reference beam's concrete batch and measured steel properties; record these in a non-admitting source crosswalk. Only after that crosswalk passes should a quarantined, source-row-preserving parser be considered. This intake performs no structural solve, fit, row admission, or validation comparison.
