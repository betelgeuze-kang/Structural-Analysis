# Predeclared large-drift design-cost comparison

The existing five-case process experiment demonstrates deterministic cost exclusion under concrete-damaging histories, but its steel accumulated plastic strain stays zero. The new optional `--history-profile large-drift` expands that development experiment's loading range. It does not assert in advance that steel yields or that the solver converges.

The fixed roster has widths 0.32 and 0.48 m, each with three-target reversing histories (-10, -20, +10) mm and (-20, -40, +20) mm. Every case retains the cantilever model, 600 kN constant axial load and the existing three unequal top/bottom reinforcement alternatives. The ordinary cases declare a synthetic maximum absolute fiber-strain screen of 0.01; other screens retain the existing development settings. These are new declared scenario limits, not a relaxation of a prior failed validation or an engineering acceptance standard. The fifth case uses the first large-drift history and a deliberately tiny 1e-12 strain limit so no feasible authority is expected.

Each case executes full then pruned processes and pruned then full processes, for five cases, ten paired observations and twenty CLI processes. Inputs and orders are written before numerical execution. Each full comparison includes the baseline plus three candidates and fresh verification; pruning requires prior verified feasibility under the same declared prices and limits. Prices are synthetic and do not support a construction savings claim.

The existing reader retains all planned cases, failed subprocesses and missing/malformed reports. Failed, unverified, differently selected or no-feasible pairs cannot receive qualified speed ratios. No aggregate across successful and unsuccessful cases is manufactured. Actual steel accumulated plastic strain and concrete damage must be read from verified full-history outputs before describing the material regime.

The original CLI profile remains the default and keeps its original scenario values. The 15-test cost-pruned-design suite passes (7.10 seconds), including complete failure retention for both profiles and generated request checks.
